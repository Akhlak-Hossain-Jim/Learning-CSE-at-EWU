## What is a Business Model?
* A business model is the way that a company sells products to its customers. [cite: 762]
* It describes the rationale of how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value. [cite: 763, 764, 781]
* It consists of 9 building blocks according to Alexander Osterwalder. [cite: 765]

## Getting from Business Idea to Business Model [cite: 767]
1. **Select the Idea:** This is your prototype solution that addresses the challenge. [cite: 768, 769, 770]
2. **Build the Model:** The business model canvas is an excellent tool for validating the new idea. [cite: 771, 772, 773]
3. **Tell the Story:** The canvas provides the key points needed to pitch your business model to others. [cite: 774, 775]

*Note: You may want to use a practice idea for confidentiality. Identify the challenge, determine the nine building blocks, and present the why, how, and what.* [cite: 776, 777, 778, 779, 780]

## The 9 Building Blocks of the Business Model Canvas

### 1. Customer Segments
* For whom are we creating value? [cite: 783]
* Who are our most important customers? [cite: 783]
* **Customer Base Types:** Is our customer base a Mass Market, Niche Market, Segmented, Diversified, or a Multi-sided Platform? [cite: 823]

### 2. Value Propositions
* What value do we deliver to the customer? [cite: 786]
* Which one of our customer's problems are we helping to solve? [cite: 786]
* Which customer needs are we satisfying? [cite: 787]
* What bundles of products and services are we offering to each Customer Segment? [cite: 787]
* **Characteristics:** Newness, Performance, Customization, "Getting the Job Done", Design, Brand/Status, Price, Cost Reduction, Risk Reduction, Accessibility, Convenience/Usability. [cite: 823]

### 3. Channels
* Through which Channels do our Customer Segments want to be reached? [cite: 789]
* How are we reaching them now? [cite: 789]
* How are our Channels integrated? [cite: 790] 
* Which ones work best and are most cost-efficient? [cite: 790]
* How are we integrating them with customer routines? [cite: 791]

### 4. Customer Relationships
* What type of relationship does each of our Customer Segments expect us to establish and maintain with them? [cite: 793]
* Which ones have we established? [cite: 794]
* How costly are they? [cite: 794]
* How are they integrated with the rest of our business model? [cite: 794]

### 5. Revenue Streams
* For what value are our customers really willing to pay? [cite: 796]
* For what do they currently pay and how are they currently paying? [cite: 796, 797]
* How would they prefer to pay? [cite: 797]
* How much does each Revenue Stream contribute to overall revenues? [cite: 798]
* **Types:** Asset sale, Usage fee, Subscription Fees, Lending/Renting/Leasing, Licensing, Brokerage fees, Advertising. [cite: 823]
* **Pricing Mechanisms:** * *Fixed Pricing:* List Price, Product feature dependent, Customer segment dependent, Volume dependent. [cite: 823]
  * *Dynamic Pricing:* Negotiation (bargaining), Yield Management, Real-time-Market. [cite: 823]

### 6. Key Resources
* What Key Resources do our Value Propositions require? [cite: 801]
* What do our Distribution Channels, Customer Relationships, and Revenue Streams require? [cite: 801]
* **Types of Resources:** Physical, Intellectual (brand patents, copyrights, data), Human, Financial. [cite: 823]

### 7. Key Activities
* What Key Activities do our Value Propositions require? [cite: 803]
* What activities do our Distribution Channels, Customer Relationships, and Revenue streams require? [cite: 803]
* **Categories:** Production, Problem Solving, Platform/Network. [cite: 823]

### 8. Key Partners
* Who are our Key Partners and key suppliers? [cite: 805]
* Which Key Resources are we acquiring from partners? [cite: 805]
* Which Key Activities do partners perform? [cite: 806]
* **Motivations for Partnerships:** Optimization and economy, Reduction of risk and uncertainty, Acquisition of particular resources and activities. [cite: 823]

### 9. Cost Structure
* What are the most important costs inherent in our business model? [cite: 808]
* Which Key Resources and Key Activities are most expensive? [cite: 808, 809]
* **Business Focus:** * *Cost Driven:* Leanest cost structure, low price value proposition, maximum automation, extensive outsourcing. [cite: 823]
  * *Value Driven:* Focused on value creation, premium value proposition. [cite: 823]
* **Sample Characteristics:** Fixed Costs (salaries, rents, utilities), Variable costs, Economies of scale, Economies of scope. [cite: 823]

## Types of Business Model [cite: 866, 867]

### 1. Business-to-Business (B2B) [cite: 868]
* This model needs two or more business organizations that do business with each other. [cite: 874]
* It entails commercial activity among companies, through the Internet as a medium. [cite: 875]
* **Types of B2B:** 1) Supplier Oriented, 2) Buyer Oriented, 3) Intermediate Oriented. [cite: 876]
* **Example:** An automobile manufacturer makes several B2B transactions such as buying tires, glass for windscreens, and rubber hoses for its vehicles. [cite: 877] The final transaction, a finished vehicle sold to the consumer, is a single B2C transaction. [cite: 878]

### 2. Business-to-Consumer (B2C) [cite: 869]
* Business or transactions conducted directly between a company and consumers who are the end-users of its products or services. [cite: 880]
* This model enables consumers to browse, select and merchandise online from a wider variety of sellers and at better prices. [cite: 881]

### 3. Consumer-to-Consumer (C2C) [cite: 870]
* Consumer to Consumer (or citizen-to-citizen) electronic commerce involves the electronically facilitated transactions between consumers through some third party. [cite: 883]
* A common example is the online auction, in which a consumer posts an item for sale and other consumers bid to purchase it. [cite: 884]

### 4. Business-to-Government (B2G) [cite: 871]
* An example of a business to government company is a firm that offers IT consulting services to a government agency. [cite: 886]
* The government uses the B2G arrangement in order to keep its technology up to date and in working condition, while at the same time limiting expenses by not taking on full-time staff. [cite: 887]

### 5. Consumer-to-Business (C2B) [cite: 872]
* Consumer-to-business (C2B) is a business model in which consumers (individuals) create value and businesses consume that value. [cite: 889]
* For example, when a consumer writes reviews or when a consumer gives a useful idea for new product development then that consumer is creating value for the business if the business adopts the input. [cite: 890]
