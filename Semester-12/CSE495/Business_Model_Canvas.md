## The Business Model Canvas (BMC)
The Business Model Canvas (BMC) is a strategic management tool that lets you visualize, design, and pivot your entire business logic on a single page. [cite: 11]

### Visualizing the Canvas
The Canvas is typically divided into sections representing the logical flow of the business:
* **The "Show":** Everything the customer sees and experiences. (Right side of the canvas) [cite: 11]
* **The "Factory":** Everything happening behind the scenes to make the show possible. (Left side of the canvas) [cite: 11]
* **The "Wallet":** What comes in vs. what goes out. (Bottom of the canvas) [cite: 11]

## The 9 Building Blocks

### 1. Customer Segments
This block defines the different groups of people or organizations an enterprise aims to reach and serve. [cite: 11] It acknowledges that customers are not a monolith; they can be grouped based on distinct needs, behaviors, attributes, or distribution channels. [cite: 11] A business model may serve one or several large or small Customer Segments. [cite: 11]

**B2C Segments (Business to Consumer):**
* **Demographic Segmentation:** Divides the market into groups based on measurable, statistical characteristics (e.g., Age, gender, income, education level, occupation, marital status, family size). [cite: 11] These variables are easily quantifiable and widely used. [cite: 11]
* **Psychographic Segmentation:** Segments customers based on intrinsic psychological and behavioral traits, including personality, values, attitudes, interests, and lifestyles (e.g., Activities, Interests, Opinions). [cite: 11] It aims to understand why consumers choose certain products. [cite: 11]
* **Behavioral Segmentation:** Groups consumers based on their knowledge of, attitude toward, use of, or response to a product. [cite: 11] Focuses on actions like Benefits Sought, Usage Rate, Loyalty Status, and Purchase Readiness. [cite: 11]

**User vs. Customer:** [cite: 11]
| Product | The User (Uses it) | The Customer (Pays for it) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Toys | The Child | The Parent |
| Google Search | The Searcher (You) | The Advertiser (Nike) |
| Corporate Slack | The Employee | The Company (IT Dept) |
| School Textbooks | The Student | The School District |

### 2. Value Proposition
The core reason why a customer chooses one company's offering over another's. [cite: 11] It is the specific bundle of products and services that creates value for a particular Customer Segment. [cite: 11] 

**The Value Proposition Canvas:** [cite: 11]
* **The Customer Profile (The Problem):** Understands the customer's world (Customer Jobs, Pains, Gains). [cite: 11]
* **The Value Map (The Solution):** Focuses on your product's offer (Products & Services, Pain Relievers, Gain Creators). [cite: 11]

**Types of Value Creation:** [cite: 11]
* **Quantitative Value (Measurable):** Price, Performance, Cost Reduction, Risk Reduction. [cite: 11]
* **Qualitative Value (Subjective):** Newness, Design/Aesthetics, Customization, "Getting the Job Done", Accessibility, Brand/Status, Convenience/Usability. [cite: 11]

### 3. Channels
Describes how a company communicates with and reaches its Customer Segments to deliver its Value Proposition. [cite: 11] The Channel must match the Customer Segment behavior and the Value Proposition (e.g., an expensive enterprise software requires a high-touch Personal Assistance channel, while a simple mobile game uses a low-touch Automated channel like the App Store). [cite: 11]

**The Five Channel Phases:** [cite: 11]
1. **Awareness:** Raising awareness (e.g., Social media campaigns, SEO). [cite: 11]
2. **Evaluation:** Helping customers evaluate (e.g., Free trials, product demos). [cite: 11]
3. **Purchase:** Allowing customers to purchase (e.g., E-commerce checkout). [cite: 11]
4. **Delivery:** Delivering the Value Proposition (e.g., Physical shipping, digital download). [cite: 11]
5. **After-sales:** Providing post-purchase support (e.g., Warranties, help desk). [cite: 11]

### 4. Customer Relationships
Describes the type of connection a business establishes with its specific Customer Segments. [cite: 11] Often driven by three main strategic goals: [cite: 11]
* **Customer Acquisition:** Attracting new users or buyers. [cite: 11]
* **Customer Retention:** Keeping existing customers and preventing churn. [cite: 11]
* **Boosting Sales (Upselling/Cross-selling):** Increasing the value of existing customers. [cite: 11]

### 5. Revenue Streams
Represents the cash a company generates from each Customer Segment. [cite: 11] It is the strategy to capture the value created. [cite: 11]

**The Two Pillars of Revenue:** [cite: 11]
* **Transactional Revenues:** Resulting from a single, one-time payment. Focuses on high volume and high-margin one-time sales (e.g., Selling a car). [cite: 11]
* **Recurring Revenues:** Resulting from ongoing payments for continued delivery. Focuses on customer retention and high Customer Lifetime Value (e.g., Subscription fees). [cite: 11]

### 6. Key Resources
Describes the most important assets required to make a business model work. [cite: 11]

**Types of Key Resources:** [cite: 11]
* **Physical Resources:** Tangible assets (e.g., Manufacturing facilities, vehicles). [cite: 11]
* **Intellectual Resources:** Non-physical proprietary assets (e.g., Brands, patents, software). [cite: 11]
* **Human Resources:** Specialized or highly talented personnel (e.g., Scientists, software engineers). [cite: 11]
* **Financial Resources:** Monetary and credit assets (e.g., Cash, lines of credit). [cite: 11]

### 7. Key Activities
Describes the most important things a company must do to make its business model work (the operational essentials). [cite: 11] Unlike Key Resources (the *things* you need), Key Activities are the *actions* that utilize those resources. [cite: 11]

### 8. Key Partners
Describes the network of suppliers, alliances, and partners essential to making the business model work. [cite: 11]

**Strategic Motivations for Partnerships:** [cite: 11]
* **Optimization and Economies of Scale:** To optimize resource allocation, often by outsourcing non-core activities (e.g., Foxconn). [cite: 11]
* **Reduction of Risk and Uncertainty:** To hedge against competitive threats or market volatility (e.g., Blu-ray Disc consortium). [cite: 11]
* **Acquisition of Resources and Activities:** To acquire specific resources or knowledge the company lacks (e.g., Partnering with Google Cloud). [cite: 11]
* **Co-opetition:** Strategic partnership between two or more competitors (e.g., Car manufacturers partnering on EV charging networks). [cite: 11]

### 9. Cost Structure
Describes all monetary expenses incurred to operate the business model. [cite: 11] It is the primary counter-balance to the Revenue Streams. [cite: 11]

**Cost Types:** [cite: 11]
* **Fixed Costs:** Expenses that remain the same regardless of volume (e.g., Salaries, rent). [cite: 11] High fixed costs mean high financial risk but enable scaling. [cite: 11]
* **Variable Costs:** Expenses that change proportionally with volume (e.g., Raw materials, shipping). [cite: 11]

**Strategic Cost Orientations:** [cite: 11]
* **Cost-Driven Models:** Minimizing costs wherever possible; often centered on low prices (e.g., Ryanair, Walmart). [cite: 11]
* **Value-Driven Models:** Creating premium value and a luxury experience, regardless of cost implications (e.g., Rolex). [cite: 11]
