## What is an Incident [cite: 7]
* An Incident can be classified as something adverse, a threat, to our computer systems or networks. [cite: 7]
* It implies harm or someone attempting to harm the organization. [cite: 7]
* Not all Incidents will be handled by an IRT ("Incident Response Team") as they do not necessarily have an impact, but those which do the IRT is summoned to help deal with the incident in a predictable and high quality manner. [cite: 7]

## What Is Incident Response? [cite: 7]
* Incident response in cyber security is a structured, strategic approach an organization uses to detect, contain, and recover from cyberattacks or security breaches. [cite: 7]
* The primary goal is to minimize damage, reduce downtime, and learn from the event to strengthen future defenses. [cite: 7]
* The goal of IR is to detect, investigate, and contain attacks within the organization. [cite: 7]
* The lessons learned from IR activities are also used to inform downstream prevention and mitigation strategies, thereby enhancing an organization's overall security posture. [cite: 7]
* The phases include: Preparation, Detection & Analysis, Containment Eradication & Recovery, and Post Incident Activity. [cite: 7]

## Why Is Incident Response Important? (Core Benefits) [cite: 7]
* **Limits Damage:** Rapid containment stops cyber threats from spreading across networks. [cite: 7]
* **Reduces Costs:** Quick recovery lowers remediation expenses and business interruption losses. [cite: 7]
* **Protects Reputation:** Fast, transparent handling preserves customer trust and brand value. [cite: 7]
* **Ensures Compliance:** Structured responses satisfy legal requirements for reporting data breaches. [cite: 7]
* **Fixes Vulnerabilities:** Post-incident reviews reveal security gaps to prevent future attacks. [cite: 7]
* **Maintains Continuity:** Pre-planned steps keep critical business operations running during crises. [cite: 7]

## What Is the Incident Response Lifecycle? [cite: 7]
* **Incident Lifecycle vs. Incident Response Lifecycle:** [cite: 7]
  * **Identify:** Process and classify alerts according to severity. [cite: 7]
  * **Investigate:** Determine if an incident occurred and gather evidence. [cite: 7]
  * **Mitigate:** Stop and respond to attacks. [cite: 7]
  * **Continuous:** Refine alerting and investigation procedures. [cite: 7]

* **7 Phases of Incident Response:** [cite: 7]
  1. **Preparing for Potential Incidents:** Define clear communication channels, implement response checklists, and provide staff with quality cybersecurity training. [cite: 7]
  2. **Identifying and Assessing Threats:** Assess whether an event is a cyber-attack, evaluate its intensity, and classify the cybersecurity incident based on the nature of the attack. [cite: 7]
  3. **Containing the Impact:** Isolate the affected systems and impede the incident from propagating further. [cite: 7]
  4. **Investigating and Eradicating Threats:** Make sure the threat is no longer present in the organization's network by investigating the root cause of the incident and eradicating any threats from the system. [cite: 7]
  5. **Recovering and Restoring Operations:** Restore the affected systems to their pre-incident state to get your business back up and running as normal. [cite: 7]
  6. **Learning from the Incident:** Document everything that occurred during the incident and the response. Use this information to recognize areas for improvement in the organization's security posture and incident response plan. [cite: 7]
  7. **Ongoing Testing and Evaluation:** Strengthen your security posture by continuously testing and evaluating your incident response plan to ensure that it remains current and effective in the face of ever-evolving cyber threats. [cite: 7]

* **An Intelligence-Driven Approach to Incident Response:** [cite: 7]
  * **Scope:** Define engagement scope. Assess the breadth, severity, and nature of the security incident. [cite: 7]
  * **Investigate:** Fully understand the incident. Experts use advanced tools for evidence collection, detection and analysis to flag IoCs, TTPs and other clues. [cite: 7]
  * **Secure:** Contain and eradicate. Remove the threat with custom eradication strategies and provide 24/7 monitoring against new malicious activity. [cite: 7]
  * **Support & Report:** Findings and response assistance. Get a detailed investigation report as well as guidance in implementing additional security controls while you get back on your feet. [cite: 7]
  * **Transform:** Improve your security posture. Use lessons learned and apply specific improvements to your security approach to protect against future and similar attacks. [cite: 7]

* **NIST Incident Response Lifecycle:** [cite: 7]
  * The incident response lifecycle is a continuous, non-linear framework established by organizations to prepare for, detect, contain, and recover from cybersecurity incidents while minimizing damage and preventing future attacks. [cite: 7]
  * **1. Preparation:** Equipping the organization with the necessary tools, policies, and trained personnel before an incident occurs. Key actions include establishing an Incident Response Plan (IRP), defining roles, setting up communication channels, and running training or simulation workshops. This ensures the team knows exactly what to do and who to contact the moment a breach happens. [cite: 7]
  * **2. Detection and Analysis:** Monitoring systems and analyzing alerts to identify precursors or indicators of a cyberattack. Key actions include using security monitoring tools to spot unusual behavior, verifying the scope of the incident, and determining the nature and impact of the threat. Rapid discovery limits the window of opportunity for threat actors. [cite: 7]
  * **3. Containment, Eradication, and Recovery:** The core operational phase where responders take direct action to stop the bleeding and restore normal operations. Containment involves isolating infected systems or blocking malicious IP addresses. Eradication involves completely removing malicious code, backdoors, or malware. Recovery involves restoring data from secure backups, rebuilding systems, and bringing services back online safely. [cite: 7]
  * **4. Post-Incident Activity (Lessons Learned):** Conducting a post-mortem review once the dust settles to understand how and why the incident occurred. Key actions include documenting a timeline of events, analyzing what went right or wrong, and updating the initial Preparation phase with new security controls to prevent the same attack vector from working again. [cite: 7]

## Introduction to Digital Forensics [cite: 7]

### What is Forensic? [cite: 7]
* Forensic science is a scientific method of gathering and examining information about the past which is then used in a court of law. [cite: 7]
* Collection and analysis of evidence. [cite: 7]
* Using scientific test or techniques. [cite: 7]
* To establish facts against crime. [cite: 7]
* For presenting in a legal proceeding. [cite: 7]

### What is Digital Forensics? [cite: 7]
* Digital Forensics is the scientific process of: Identifying, Preserving, Analyzing, and Presenting. [cite: 7]
* Digital forensics involves the identification, preservation, collection, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence in a legally admissible manner. [cite: 7]
* **Core Components:** [cite: 7]
  * **Scientific Process:** Methodical approach to evidence handling. [cite: 7]
  * **Legal Framework:** Adherence to rules of evidence. [cite: 7]
  * **Technical Expertise:** Understanding digital systems and data. [cite: 7]
  * **Documentation:** Comprehensive recording of all actions. [cite: 7]
  * **Purpose:** To investigate digital incidents while maintaining evidence integrity for legal proceedings. [cite: 7]

### Why Digital Forensics is Important? [cite: 7]
1. Investigate cyber crimes. [cite: 7]
2. Incident response. [cite: 7]
3. Insider threat detection. [cite: 7]
4. Data breach investigation. [cite: 7]
5. Legal dispute resolution. [cite: 7]

### Objectives of Digital Forensics [cite: 7]
* **Evidence to Court:** It recovers, analyzes, and preserves digital and forensic evidence to help the department's investigation to present the evidence in court. [cite: 7]
* **Identifying the Culprit:** It aims to cause the attacks and identify the main culprit behind the crimes. [cite: 7]
* **Legal Procedures:** To ensure the evidence found at a suspicious crime scene is uncorrupted, we design the methods for collecting and preserving the evidence. [cite: 7]
* **Data Redundancy:** Recover the deleted files and subdivide them from digital media to validate them. [cite: 7]
* It also encourages you to find the evidence instantly and makes you identify the impact of the culprit on the crime or the attacks. [cite: 7]
* Storing the evidence or the proofs by the procedures in the way of legal custody in the court of law. [cite: 7]

## The Process of Digital Forensics [cite: 7]
* **Identification:** Understand and clearly define the purpose of the investigation. Pin pointing the potential source of digital evidence (What evidence is present, Where it is stored, and How it is stored). Electronic stores can be: Personal computers, Mobile phones, PDAs, Smart cards. [cite: 7]
* **Preservation:** Isolate, secure, and preserve data to prevent tampering. This includes preventing people from using the digital device or allowing other electromagnetic devices to be used within an affected radius. [cite: 7]
* **Analysis / Extraction & Analysis:** Carefully examine and interpret the preserved digital evidence. Employing specialized techniques to retrieve data from devices. Determine significance, reconstruct fragments of data and draw conclusions based on evidence found. It may take several iterations of examination and analysis to support a crime theory. [cite: 7]
* **Documentation:** Comprehensive record-keeping including a detailed account of actions. A record of all visible data must be created, which helps in recreating the scene and reviewing it any time. Involves proper documentation of the crime scene along with photographing, sketching and crime-scene mapping. [cite: 7]
* **Presentation:** Summarize and explain the conclusions drawn from the analysis. Compiling and communicating findings to relevant stakeholders, requires expert testimony. This should be written in layperson's terms using abstracted terminologies, which should reference specific details. [cite: 7]

## Primary Domains & Branches of Digital Forensics [cite: 7]
* **Computer Forensics:** Desktops, laptops, servers. Involves extracting and analyzing data from non-volatile storage, such as desktops, laptops, and hard drives, to recover deleted files, system logs, and user activity, often used to trace data theft or unauthorized access. [cite: 7]
* **Network Forensics:** Traffic analysis, intrusion detection. Focuses on monitoring and analyzing network traffic to identify, track, or intercept unauthorized access, intrusions (e.g., DDoS attacks), or data exfiltration. [cite: 7]
* **Mobile Forensics:** Smartphones, tablets, GPS devices. Specializes in retrieving data from mobile devices like smartphones, tablets, and wearable devices, including SMS messages, call logs, location data (GPS), and app usage. [cite: 7]
* **Database Forensics:** Data manipulation investigation. Relates to the forensic study and collection of databases and their relevant metadata. [cite: 7]
* **Cloud Forensics:** Virtualized environments and services. Involves the investigation of data within cloud environments (SaaS, IaaS, PaaS), including virtual machines, AWS, Azure, and GCP, focusing on API activities and access logs. [cite: 7]
* **Memory Forensics:** Analyzes volatile data in a system's RAM, such as encryption keys, running processes, and network connections, often crucial for finding evidence that disappears when the machine shuts down. [cite: 7]
* **Disk Forensics:** Deriving evidence from digital storage media like USB Devices, DVDs, CDs, etc. [cite: 7]
* **Wireless Forensics:** A part of networking forensics targeting wireless traffic. [cite: 7]
* **Malware Forensics:** Handles identifying malicious code and studying malware issues. [cite: 7]
* **Email Forensics:** Handles the recovery of trashed data and analyses email contents, calendars, and contacts. [cite: 7]

## Digital Forensic Core & Evidence [cite: 7]
* **Digital evidence** is any data that is recorded or preserved on any medium in or by a computer system or other similar digital device, that can be read or understood by a person or a computer system or other similar device. It includes a display, printout or other output of that data. [cite: 7]
* Never tamper with the original drive. Snap a clean copy of virtual systems. "Live" evidence can disappear in an instant. [cite: 7]
* **An evidence must be:** [cite: 7]
  * **Admissible:** Conformity with the common law and legislative rules. [cite: 7]
  * **Authentic:** In linking data to specific individuals and events. [cite: 7]
  * **Fragile:** Easily altered, damaged, or destroyed. [cite: 7]
  * **Accurate:** Believed and is consistent. [cite: 7]
  * **Complete:** With a full story of particular circumstances. [cite: 7]

* **Types of Digital Evidence:** [cite: 7]
  * **Persistent data:** Data that remains intact when the digital device is turned off (e.g., hard drives, disk drives, USB drives). [cite: 7]
  * **Volatile data:** Data that would be lost if the digital device is turned off (e.g., deleted files, computer history, registry, temporary files, RAM). [cite: 7]

## Forensic Principles & Modeling [cite: 7]
* **Core Forensic Principles:** [cite: 7]
  * **Integrity:** Evidence must remain unaltered. Investigators use hash values (digital fingerprints) and write-blockers to prove the data hasn't changed. [cite: 7]
  * **Repeatability:** Different examiners using the same tools on the same data must reach the same results. [cite: 7]
  * **Documentation:** A meticulous "paper trail" of every action, tool, and command used during the investigation. [cite: 7]
  * **Chain of Custody:** A chronological log of who handled the evidence, when, and why. Any gap can disqualify the evidence. [cite: 7]
  * **Legal Compliance:** All actions must follow the law (warrants, privacy rights) to ensure findings are admissible in court. [cite: 7]

* **Common Forensic Process Models:** [cite: 7]
  * **DFRWS Model:** Identification -> Preservation -> Collection -> Examination -> Analysis -> Presentation -> Decision. [cite: 7]
  * **NIST SP 800-86 Model:** Collection -> Examination -> Analysis -> Reporting. [cite: 7]
  * **Abstract Digital Forensics Model (ADFM):** Preparation -> Collection -> Preservation -> Examination -> Analysis -> Presentation -> Dissemination. [cite: 7]
  * **Integrated Digital Investigation Process (IDIP):** Readiness -> Deployment -> Physical Crime Scene -> Digital Crime Scene -> Review. [cite: 7]

* **Digital Forensic Investigation Model:** Identification -> Preservation -> Collection -> Examination -> Analysis -> Presentation. [cite: 7]

## Skills and Challenges [cite: 7]
* **Skills Required to Become a Digital Forensic Investigator:** [cite: 7]
  * Defeating anti-forensic techniques. [cite: 7]
  * Understanding hard disks and file systems. [cite: 7]
  * Operating system forensics. [cite: 7]
  * Cloud forensics in a cloud environment. [cite: 7]
  * Investigating email crimes. [cite: 7]
  * Mobile device forensics. [cite: 7]
* **Challenges:** [cite: 7]
  * **Encryption:** Ubiquitous hardware-backed encryption and End-to-End Encryption (E2EE) make data unreadable without a passcode. Investigators often must shift to "live" memory forensics to capture keys. [cite: 7]
  * **Anti-forensic techniques:** Criminals use tools to actively erase traces, such as artifact wiping and steganography. Many modern attacks are "fileless," living only in RAM. [cite: 7]
  * Increase of PCs and internet access makes exchange of information quick and inexpensive. [cite: 7]
  * Easy availability of Hacking Tools. [cite: 7]
  * Lack of physical evidence. [cite: 7]
  * Large amount of storage space available to suspects. [cite: 7]
  * Rapid technological changes require constant upgrades. [cite: 7]
  * Cloud data jurisdiction, Large-scale data analysis, AI-generated cyber attacks. [cite: 7]
