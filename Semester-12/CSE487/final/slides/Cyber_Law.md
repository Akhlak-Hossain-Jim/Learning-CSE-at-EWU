## 1. Overview of Cyber Law & Governance

**Definition:** Cyber Law (also termed Internet Law) is the comprehensive legal framework governing internet operations, digital telecommunications, computing hardware, electronic data privacy, and online transactions.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            KEY DOMAINS OF CYBER LAW                               |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Cybercrime           | Hacking, malware propagation, identity theft, DDoS      |
| 2. Data Privacy         | Personal data governance, cross-border flows (GDPR)     |
| 3. Intellectual Property| Copyright, software piracy, patents, digital media      |
| 4. Electronic Commerce  | Smart contracts, digital signatures, consumer rights    |
| 5. National Security    | Critical information infrastructure & cyber terrorism   |
| 6. Freedom of Speech    | Balancing public expression against online defamation   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 2. Core Cybercrime Categories & Protection Mechanisms

### Major Cybercrime Classifications:
* **DDoS Attacks & Botnets:** Overwhelming online infrastructure to disrupt service availability.
* **Identity Theft & Social Engineering:** Stealing digital identities, credentials, or PII via phishing and impersonation.
* **Cyberstalking & Online Harassment:** Systematic harassment, surveillance, or intimidation of individuals online.
* **Potentially Unwanted Programs (PUPs) & Malware:** Unauthorized distribution of trojans, spyware, and ransomware.
* **Software Piracy:** Unauthorized duplication, sharing, or unlicensed installation of copyrighted software.

### Cyber Insurance Protection Coverage:
* **Direct Financial Loss:** Coverage for losses from phishing, invoice redirection, and email spoofing.
* **Fraudulent Transactions:** Protection against unauthorized electronic banking or card transfers.
* **System Restoration:** Reimbursement for costs to clean malware and rebuild corrupted databases.
* **Extortion Losses:** Coverage for forensic investigation and ransom negotiations under legal oversight.
* **Legal Liability & Defense:** Legal representation and damages arising from privacy lawsuits.

## 4. Professional Ethics & Incident Response for Practitioners

### Professional Code of Conduct (Do's & Don'ts):

| Category | Ethical Practice (Do's) | Prohibited Conduct (Don'ts) |
|---|---|---|
| **Data Privacy** | Access sensitive data only with written authorization. | Never use internal privileged data for private profit. |
| **System Integrity** | Perform routine security patching and audits. | Never deploy unvetted, pirated, or cracked software. |
| **User Safety** | Educate end-users on phishing and threat hygiene. | Do not fall for online lottery/prize schemes or click suspicious links. |
| **Accountability** | Acknowledge errors and correct system defects promptly. | Never shift blame to colleagues or conceal breaches. |

### Statutory Duty of Due Diligence (When Compromised):
1. **Preserve Forensic Evidence:** Retain uncorrupted server access logs, network packet captures, and memory dumps.
2. **Statutory Reporting:** Promptly notify law enforcement bodies and regulatory authorities (e.g., BGD e-Gov CIRT).
3. **Due Diligence Obligation:** Organizations maintain legal liability even when victimized; failure to exercise reasonable cybersecurity diligence can result in severe civil regulatory penalties.
