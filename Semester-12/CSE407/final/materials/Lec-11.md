## The Green IT transformation lifecycle
*Foundation · 10 min*
A structured 4-phase framework for enterprise-wide implementation

- A successful transformation is not a single project; it is an iterative lifecycle. The standard enterprise framework spans four distinct phases designed to move an organization from reactive compliance to proactive leadership.
- Phase 1 — Assess & Plan — Baselining current footprint & gap analysis
- Phase 2 — Architect & Implement — Deploying quick wins & core infrastructure
- Phase 3 — Measure & Optimize — Continuous KPI tracking & refinement
- Phase 4 — Institutionalize — Embedding policies into organizational culture
- **📋 Phase 1 & 2: Baseline to Implementation**
- Establish Scope 1, 2, and 3 energy baselines — Perform Maturity Model assessment (e.g., CMMI-aligned Green IT models) — Identify low-hanging fruit (consolidation, PC power management) — Execute green procurement requirements for hardware
- **🔄 Phase 3 & 4: Optimization to Governance**
- Implement automated DCIM and IoT monitoring dashboards — Link Green IT KPIs to executive and department performance reviews — Establish formal e-waste and recycling vendor audits — Publish annual ESG and sustainability reports

> **⚠️ The "Set and Forget" Risk**
> Without Phase 4 (Institutionalization), efficiency gains erode over time. Staff turn over, legacy hardware creeps back in, and virtualization ratios degrade without active enforcement.

## Change management & cultural alignment
*Organizational Behavior · 15 min*
Overcoming human resistance, incentives, and Kotter’s 8-Step Model

- Technology accounts for only 20% of transformation failure; human factors account for the other 80%. Aligning organizational culture requires deliberate change management models like Kotter’s 8-Step Framework applied to sustainability.
- **Steps 1 & 2**
- **Create Urgency & Build a Guiding Coalition** — Aligning C-suite and cross-functional leaders
- **Urgency Drivers** — Highlight rising energy tariffs, regulatory compliance penalties, and corporate reputation risks.
- **Guiding Coalition** — Form a Green Steering Committee combining IT, Facilities, Finance, and HR leadership.
- **Steps 3 & 4**
- **Develop Vision & Communicate Buy-In** — Translating goals into clear staff behaviors
- **Green Vision** — Set tangible targets: "30% energy reduction in 24 months with zero performance compromise."
- **Communication** — Use departmental dashboards, town halls, and internal green champion networks.
- **Steps 5 & 6**
- **Empower Action & Generate Quick Wins** — Removing friction and demonstrating ROI fast
- **Empowerment** — Remove policies that force paper printing or require always-on desktop hardware.
- **Quick Wins** — Execute rapid server virtualization or PC sleep policy rollouts to show immediate $ savings.
- **Steps 7 & 8**
- **Sustain Acceleration & Anchor in Culture** — Making Green IT second nature across the organization
- **Sustain Gains** — Reinvest energy cost savings into next-gen sustainable technologies and tools.
- **Cultural Anchor** — Embed green criteria into hiring, employee onboarding, and annual performance reviews.

## IT governance, compliance & policies
*Policy & Standards · 10 min*
ISO 14001, GHG Protocol, and green procurement policies

- Governance provides the legal and procedural guardrails for transformation. Standard compliance frameworks help organizations avoid greenwashing while meeting regulatory auditing requirements.
- 📜 **ISO 14001 Standards** — Framework for Environmental Management Systems (EMS) to continuously measure and improve environmental impacts.
- 🏭 **GHG Protocol (Scope 1, 2, 3)** — Standard accounting for direct emissions (Scope 1), purchased electricity (Scope 2), and supply chain/vendor footprint (Scope 3).
- 🛒 **Sustainable Procurement Policy** — Mandating Energy Star, EPEAT Gold, or TCO Certified ratings for all new hardware acquisitions.
- ♻️ **E-Waste Governance (WEEE)** — Certified chain-of-custody recycling to ensure zero hazardous electronic waste ends up in landfills.

## Math practice — small, quick calculations
*Practice · 10 min*
Apply transformation metrics (payback, TCO savings, carbon emissions) to five short problems

- Solve each problem on paper first, then tap a card to reveal the step-by-step solution.
- **1** — An enterprise invests **$60,000** in dynamic power management software. It cuts annual electricity usage by **200,000 kWh**. If electricity costs **$0.15/kWh**, what is the simple payback period?
- Savings = kWh × Cost; Payback = CapEx ÷ Savings — 200,000 × $0.15 = $30,000/yr
$60,000 ÷ $30,000 = 2.0 Years — A 2-year payback is considered excellent for software-driven Green IT projects.
- **2** — A company replaces 100 legacy servers (400W each) with 10 virtualized host servers (600W each), running 24/7 for 1 year (8,760 hrs). How many **kWh** are saved annually?
- Power Saved (kW) × 8,760 Hours — Legacy: 100 × 0.4kW = 40 kW
New: 10 × 0.6kW = 6 kW
Saved: (40 - 6) × 8,760 = 297,840 kWh — Virtualization delivers nearly 300 MWh of annual energy reduction in this scenario.
- **3** — If Bangladesh's electricity grid carbon intensity factor is **0.6 kg CO₂e per kWh**, how many metric tons of CO₂e are offset by saving **150,000 kWh**?
- Tons CO₂e = (kWh × Factor) ÷ 1,000 — (150,000 × 0.6) ÷ 1,000 = 90 Metric Tons — This direct conversion is crucial for mandatory corporate Scope 2 carbon reporting.
- **4** — A legacy PC fleet draws **50 kW** during non-working hours due to disabled sleep policies. Enforcing night sleep cuts this off-hours draw to **5 kW** across 12 off-peak hours per day. How much energy is saved per day?
- Daily kWh Saved = ΔkW × Off-peak Hours — (50 kW - 5 kW) × 12 hrs = 540 kWh / day — Over a 250-day working year, that simple policy tweak saves 135,000 kWh with $0 CapEx!
- **5** — An enterprise spends **$100,000** on a green data center retrofit. Over 5 years, it saves **$35,000** per year in electricity. What is the 5-Year Net ROI percentage?
- ROI% = [(Total Savings - CapEx) ÷ CapEx] × 100 — 5-Yr Savings: $35,000 × 5 = $175,000
Net Return: $175,000 - $100,000 = $75,000
ROI: ($75,000 ÷ $100,000) × 100 = +75% — A +75% net return over 5 years easily secures CFO project authorization.

## Green IT transformation in Bangladesh
*Local Context · 10 min*
Grid tariffs, SREDA policies, and banking sector mandates

- Transformation drivers in Bangladesh are heavily influenced by commercial electricity price hikes, national energy shortages, and strict green banking guidelines from Bangladesh Bank.
- **⚡ Rising electricity tariffs** — Commercial power tariffs in Bangladesh have risen steadily. Energy efficiency in server rooms directly impacts bottom-line operating margins for tech firms and commercial banks in Dhaka.
- **🏦 Bangladesh Bank Green Guidelines** — Central bank regulations require commercial banks to allocate specific funds to green finance, implement in-house green office policies, and report annual Scope 1 & 2 footprints.
- **Corporate scenario** — A commercial bank in Dhaka wants to reduce branch IT energy costs by 25%. What is the most effective policy-first initiative?
- Answer — Centralized automated PC power management combined with thin-client deployment in branches. This cuts per-terminal power draw from 150W to 15W while enforcing off-hours shutdown.
- **Regulatory scenario** — How does SREDA (Sustainable & Renewable Energy Development Authority) influence corporate Green IT in Bangladesh?
- Answer — SREDA provides energy audit standards, guidelines for rooftop solar integration for data centers, and awards certification for energy-efficient commercial buildings.

**Q2. Which GHG Protocol scope covers the indirect carbon emissions resulting from an organization’s purchased electricity?**
▫️ A. Scope 1
✅ B. Scope 2
▫️ C. Scope 3
▫️ D. Scope 4
> **Explanation:** Scope 1 covers direct emissions (e.g., diesel generators). Scope 2 covers indirect emissions from purchased electricity, steam, heating, or cooling. Scope 3 covers supply chain and vendor activities.
